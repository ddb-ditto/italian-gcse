standfirst: "Every Italian noun is masculine or feminine, and the word for <em>the</em> changes to match. Four sessions, and the third is the one that matters."
canDos:
  - "Say whether a noun is masculine or feminine, and say what told them"
  - "Put il or la in front of any noun ending in -o or -a, without stopping to think"
  - "Use l' before a vowel, and still know which gender the noun is"
  - "Explain why it is lo zucchero and not il zucchero, in their own words"
  - "Name a thing in the house with its article, first time, without being asked twice"
insist: "The fourth. Every learner ends up able to <em>copy</em> <em>lo</em>; the ones who can say why it exists stop getting it wrong two years later."
contingency:
  - "<strong>If gender feels arbitrary, stop defending it.</strong> It is arbitrary. <em>La sedia</em> is not feminine for a reason a child can feel. Say so plainly — it is a label the language attaches to a word, and the job is to learn it attached, which is why every noun is learned with its article rather than on its own."
  - "<strong>Do not let them learn the noun without the article.</strong> A child who has learned <em>sedia</em> has to do two things to say it. A child who learned <em>la sedia</em> has to do one. If they are saying the noun and then reaching for the article, go back to Session 1 and relearn the words as pairs."
  - "<strong>If <em>lo</em> will not stick, make it a sound problem again.</strong> Have them say <em>il studente</em> aloud, fast, three times. It is genuinely awkward in the mouth. That awkwardness is the entire reason the word exists, and feeling it works better than being told it."

<Section kicker={"Before you start"}>What this unit is for</Section>

<p>The notebook and pen from Unit 1, and the same recorder. Nothing new to get.</p>

<p>This is the first unit with vocabulary in it. Every word here is on the examined list, and every
one is learned <strong>with its article attached</strong> — never <em>sedia</em>, always
<em>la sedia</em>. That single habit is worth more over two years than any amount of explanation,
because it turns a two-part decision into one remembered thing.</p>

<Note lead={"Why the article is the hard part"}>
<p>English has one word for <em>the</em>. Italian has seven, and which one you need depends on the
gender of the noun and on the sound it starts with. Gender has to be memorised. The sound part is a
rule — and it is the same kind of rule as Unit 1's hard and soft c, which is why this unit comes
directly after that one.</p>
</Note>

<Warn lead={"Singular only"}>
<p>Nothing in this unit is plural. <em>I</em>, <em>gli</em> and <em>le</em> are Unit 3, and bringing
them forward turns four clean rules into eleven muddled ones. If they ask what happens with two
chairs, say "that is next time" and mean it.</p>
</Warn>

<Notebook lead={"In the notebook"}>
<p><strong>Leave the fifth page empty.</strong> Unit 1's sound rules are on the first four; the
article table from this unit goes on the fifth, and the two of them together are the reference the
next two years are built on.</p>
<p>Every new noun goes in with its article and nothing else — no English, no picture. The English
belongs on the flashcard, where it can be turned over. In the notebook the pair is the point.</p>
</Notebook>
