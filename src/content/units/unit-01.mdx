<Section kicker={"Before you start"}>What you need, and why this unit exists</Section>

<p>Nothing but a voice recorder — a phone is fine — and these pages. No book, no worksheets.</p>

<p>Italian spelling maps onto sound almost perfectly. Unlike French or English, once you know about a
dozen rules you can pronounce any written word correctly on sight, forever. That is a genuinely
unusual gift, and it is worth five sessions at the very start, because everything afterwards —
vocabulary, listening, speaking — gets easier if the sound system is already in place.</p>

<Notebook lead={"A notebook each"}>
<p>They write as well as speak, from the first session. It is not a substitute for saying things
  aloud — it is how a rule stops being something you told them and becomes something they own, and it is
  Italian handwriting practice, which is examined in two of the four papers.</p>
  <p><strong>Leave the first four pages empty.</strong> The sound rules from this unit — the vowel
  table, the hard and soft rule, the stress rule — get copied there, and stay findable for two years.
  Everything after that runs in order, with every entry dated.</p>
  <p>Each session says what goes in the notebook. Where a word is written, it is said aloud at the same
  time. Silent copying is handwriting practice and nothing else.</p>
</Notebook>

<Note lead={"Hold the line on this"}>
<p>Do <strong>not</strong> teach the meaning of any word in this unit beyond the greetings in
  Session 5. If they are decoding meaning, they are not listening to sound.</p>
</Note>

<Note lead={"Your own preparation"}>
<p>You need to be able to make these sounds too. If you are unsure of any of them, look the word up
  on Forvo or Google Translate's audio before the session. Your pronunciation is the model they will
  copy, so it is worth five minutes.</p>
</Note>

<Section kicker={"The unit"}>Five sessions</Section>

<p>Each session is a single sitting with its own page and its own set of flashcards. Work through them
in order. The cards for a session only ever cover what that session taught.</p>

<Section kicker={"Before Unit 2"}>Can-do check</Section>

<p>Move on when these are true, not when the material has been covered.</p>

<Plain>
<li>Pronounce all five vowels cleanly, without sliding</li>
  <li>Read an unfamiliar word aloud and get it broadly right</li>
  <li>Explain when c and g are hard or soft, in their own words</li>
  <li>Hear the difference between <em>nonno</em> and <em>nono</em></li>
  <li>Exchange greetings and names without prompting</li>
</Plain>

<p class="noprint">Record who has met each of these in
the progress tracker in the main folder.</p>

<Note lead={"The one to insist on"}>
<p>The third. If they can state the rule, they own it. If they can only follow it when you remind
  them, they don't yet.</p>
</Note>

<Section kicker={"Contingency"}>If it's going badly</Section>

<Plain>
<li><strong>Don't stall on <em>gli</em>.</strong> It defeats a lot of learners. Mark it "to revisit", accept an approximation, move on. It will come.</li>
  <li><strong>Do stall on double consonants.</strong> If they can't hear the difference between <em>nonno</em> and <em>nono</em>, repeat Session 3 rather than progressing. Everything downstream — listening comprehension especially — depends on hearing that distinction.</li>
  <li><strong>If a child is rushing</strong>, slow the whole session down rather than telling them to slow down. Say your own lines more slowly than feels natural; they will match your pace without being asked, which works better than instruction.</li>
</Plain>
