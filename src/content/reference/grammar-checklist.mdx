<Warn lead={"The most useful thing in the appendix, and the easiest to miss"}>
<p>A large part of what separates the two tiers is not new grammar at all — it is the same grammar
  moving from recognise-only to must-produce. Teach those items once, properly, and the tier decision
  gets much cheaper to make late.</p>
</Warn>

<Warn lead={"Two Foundation limits worth knowing"}>
<p><strong>1.</strong> The imperfect at Foundation is productive only for <em>avere</em>, <em>essere</em>,
  <em>stare</em> and <em>fare</em>. Other verbs in the imperfect are receptive at that tier.<br />
  <strong>2.</strong> The conditional at Foundation means <em>vorrei</em> and <em>mi piacerebbe</em>.
  That is the whole requirement. The full conditional is Higher.</p>
  <p>Neither means "don't teach it" — the role play explicitly invites a conditional where it sounds
  natural, and both children will need the imperfect properly if Higher is in play. But it tells you
  where the Foundation floor sits if the tier decision goes the other way.</p>
</Warn>

<Section kicker={"Caveats"}>Notes</Section>

<Plain>
<li>The appendix says its bracketed examples are indicative rather than exhaustive, and that students at both tiers will meet familiar words and forms that aren't on any list. <strong>Treat every list here as a floor.</strong></li>
  <li>Issue 5 of the spec prints the plural of <em>il dito</em> as <em>la dita</em>. It should be <em>le dita</em>. Worth knowing so nobody learns the typo.</li>
  <li>The subjunctive is a smaller ask than it looks: present tense only, in set expressions, and the past subjunctive is recognition only. <em>Penso che sia</em>, <em>credo che abbia</em> and a handful of others cover most of what's needed.</li>
</Plain>

<Section kicker={"Appendix 3"}>Vocabulary — how it's organised</Section>

<p>Not reproduced here. It runs from page 75 to 132 of the spec and is better used from the PDF than

<p><strong>Section 1, high-frequency language</strong> cuts across every theme, so it should be drilled
continuously from week one rather than assigned to a term: common verbs, adjectives, adverbs,
prepositions; colours; numbers and ordinals; quantities and measures; connecting words; time
expressions, times of day, days, months and seasons; question words; countries, continents,
nationalities, geographical areas; acronyms; social conventions; and the language of dialogue and
messages.</p>

<Warn lead={"The single highest-value section in the appendix"}>
<p>The verb list alone is several hundred entries. Working through it steadily across year one would
  be time better spent than almost anything else.</p>
</Warn>

<p><strong>Section 2, topic-specific language</strong> is organised under the same five themes and topics
as the course, so it maps directly onto the term plan. The spec notes that words are listed under one
heading but are not exclusive to it, so expect to meet the same vocabulary across several themes.</p>
