<Section kicker={"Before anything else"}>How to use this</Section>

<p class="lede">Units, not weeks. A unit is a chunk of material, not a length of time.</p>

<p>Some will take three days, some three weeks, and two children will not move at the same rate —
one may cover two units while the other consolidates one. That is the design, not a failure of it.</p>

<p>Each unit has four parts:</p>

<WordTable words={[{"term": "Grammar", "gloss": "the structure being introduced"}, {"term": "Language", "gloss": "the vocabulary field it lives in"}, {"term": "Can-do", "gloss": "what they should be able to do at the end"}, {"term": "Task", "gloss": "something produced, spoken or written"}]} />

<Warn lead={"Move on when the can-do is true"}>
<p>Not when the material has been covered. The distinction matters more in languages than in most
  subjects.</p>
</Warn>

<h3>Two rules worth holding to</h3>
<Steps>
<li><strong>Record the speaking tasks from Unit 1.</strong> Not to assess them — just so there is a trail. Progress in pronunciation is invisible week to week and obvious across six months.</li>
  <li><strong>Recycle relentlessly.</strong> Every unit should reuse the previous three. Italian grammar compounds; nothing is ever done.</li>
</Steps>

<p>If they arrive with some Italian already, use Unit 1 as a diagnostic and skip whatever is genuinely
secure. Don't skip Units 2 and 3 — gender and plural formation underpin every agreement rule that
follows, and they are the most common source of errors that persist for years.</p>

<Section kicker={"Block A"}>Sound and substance · Units 1–4</Section>

<h3><a href="../Unit 01 — The sound of it/00 — Unit 1 introduction.html">Unit 1 — The sound of it</a></h3>
<div class="spec">
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Grammar</span></div><div>None yet. Pronunciation and stress only.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Language</span></div><div>Alphabet; greetings; <em>ciao</em> / <em>buongiorno</em> / <em>buonasera</em>; <em>come ti chiami</em>, <em>mi chiamo</em>; <em>come stai</em>; <em>per favore</em>, <em>grazie</em>, <em>prego</em>.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Can-do</span></div><div>Read any written Italian word aloud and pronounce it correctly, even without knowing what it means.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Task</span></div><div>Record both children reading a short list of unfamiliar words. Keep it. This is the baseline everything is measured against.</div></div>
</div>
<Note>
<p>Italian is close to phonetic, which makes this achievable in one unit and worth every minute.
  Spend the time on: c and g before e/i versus a/o/u; ch and gh; gli and gn; sc and sch; double
  consonants, which are genuinely different sounds and not decoration; and where the stress falls.</p>
</Note>

<h3>Unit 2 — Naming things</h3>
<div class="spec">
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Grammar</span></div><div>Noun gender. Definite and indefinite articles, singular (<em>il, lo, l', la</em> / <em>un, uno, un'</em>). Why <em>lo</em> and <em>uno</em> exist.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Language</span></div><div>Classroom and household objects; rooms of the house.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Can-do</span></div><div>Name an object with the correct article without hesitating.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Task</span></div><div>Label things around the house with sticky notes, article included. Leave them up for a month.</div></div>
</div>

<h3>Unit 3 — More than one</h3>
<div class="spec">
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Grammar</span></div><div>Plural formation. Plural articles (<em>i, gli, le</em>). The common irregulars from the spec: <em>la mano</em>, <em>il cinema</em>, <em>il/la ciclista</em>, <em>la foto</em>, <em>gli uomini</em>.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Language</span></div><div>Same field as Unit 2, extended.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Can-do</span></div><div>Pluralise any regular noun and its article on sight.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Task</span></div><div>Take twenty singular nouns, convert them, then convert them back.</div></div>
</div>

<h3>Unit 4 — To be, and to have</h3>
<div class="spec">
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Grammar</span></div><div><em>Essere</em> and <em>avere</em>, all six persons. Subject pronouns and why Italian usually omits them. Negatives with <em>non</em>. Forming questions by intonation alone.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Language</span></div><div>Nationalities; ages; how you're feeling; <em>ho fame</em>, <em>ho sete</em>, <em>ho caldo</em>, <em>ho freddo</em>.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Can-do</span></div><div>Say who they are, how old they are, where they're from — and ask the same of someone else.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Task</span></div><div>A two-minute recorded exchange between the two of them. First time they've done anything conversational; expect it to be stilted.</div></div>
</div>

<Section kicker={"Block B"}>Describing and doing · Units 5–9</Section>

<h3>Unit 5 — Describing</h3>
<div class="spec">
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Grammar</span></div><div>Adjective agreement, all four endings. Adjective position, and the small set that shifts meaning depending where it sits.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Language</span></div><div>Appearance and personality; colours; family members.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Can-do</span></div><div>Describe a person in three or four accurate sentences.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Task</span></div><div>Describe every member of the family in writing, pets included. Roughly 40 words each.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Links</span></div><div>Theme 1 — what my friends and family are like; what makes a good friend.</div></div>
</div>

<h3>Unit 6 — The present tense, part one</h3>
<div class="spec">
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Grammar</span></div><div>Regular <em>-are</em> verbs, all six persons.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Language</span></div><div>Daily routine; household chores; school subjects.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Can-do</span></div><div>Describe an ordinary day using six or more different verbs.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Task</span></div><div>Write out a typical Saturday. Then say it aloud from memory, recorded.</div></div>
</div>

<h3>Unit 7 — The present tense, part two</h3>
<div class="spec">
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Grammar</span></div><div>Regular <em>-ere</em> and <em>-ire</em> verbs, including the <em>-isc-</em> group (<em>capire</em>, <em>finire</em>, <em>preferire</em>) and why it exists.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Language</span></div><div>Interests and hobbies; sport; music; reading.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Can-do</span></div><div>Talk about what they do in their free time and what they prefer.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Task</span></div><div>A recorded minute each on free time. No notes.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Links</span></div><div>Theme 1 — interests; sport; music; reading.</div></div>
</div>

<h3>Unit 8 — The verbs that refuse to behave</h3>
<div class="spec">
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Grammar</span></div><div><em>Andare</em>, <em>fare</em>, <em>stare</em>, <em>venire</em>, <em>uscire</em>, <em>dare</em>, <em>dire</em>. <em>Mi piace</em> and <em>mi piacciono</em> — including why the thing liked is the subject, which is worth getting right now rather than unpicking later.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Language</span></div><div>Going out; weekend activities; likes and dislikes with reasons.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Can-do</span></div><div>Express a preference and justify it with <em>perché</em>.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Task</span></div><div>Five opinions, each with a reason. Spoken, then written.</div></div>
</div>
<Warn lead={"Start the habit here"}>
<p>Justified opinion is rewarded in every single speaking and writing mark grid in this
  qualification. From this unit on, never accept an unjustified opinion again.</p>
</Warn>

<h3>Unit 9 — Whose, and which</h3>
<div class="spec">
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Grammar</span></div><div>Possessive adjectives, short and long forms, including the family-member exception. Demonstratives <em>questo</em> and <em>quello</em>. Interrogative adjectives <em>qual</em>, <em>quale</em>, <em>quali</em>.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Language</span></div><div>Family in depth; relationships; possessions.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Can-do</span></div><div>Ask and answer about who belongs to whom and what belongs to whom.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Task</span></div><div>Bring a photograph. Describe who everyone is, recorded.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Links</span></div><div>Theme 1 — relationships; socialising with friends and family.</div></div>
</div>

<Section kicker={"Block C"}>Placing and counting · Units 10–12</Section>

<h3>Unit 10 — Where things are</h3>
<div class="spec">
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Grammar</span></div><div>Common prepositions (<em>a, da, di, in, su, con, per, tra/fra, senza, entro</em>). Contracted forms with the definite article — drill these to automaticity, they appear in every paper. Compound prepositions: <em>vicino a</em>, <em>lontano da</em>, <em>senza di</em>.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Language</span></div><div>The town; local area; buildings and places; directions.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Can-do</span></div><div>Give directions and say where something is.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Task</span></div><div>Describe the route from your house to somewhere local, in writing, then aloud.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Links</span></div><div>Theme 2 — town, region and country; directions.</div></div>
</div>

<h3>Unit 11 — Numbers, clocks and calendars</h3>
<div class="spec">
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Grammar</span></div><div>Cardinal and ordinal numbers. Telling the time. Dates. Adverbs of quantity: <em>abbastanza, molto, poco, un po', tanto, troppo</em>. Adverbs of time and place: <em>oggi, domani, qui, qua</em>.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Language</span></div><div>Timetables; daily schedule; days, months, seasons; birthdays.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Can-do</span></div><div>Say when anything happens, at what time, on what date.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Task</span></div><div>Write out a week's schedule in Italian and talk through it.</div></div>
</div>

<h3>Unit 12 — Who you're talking to</h3>
<div class="spec">
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Grammar</span></div><div><em>Tu</em>, <em>voi</em> and <em>Lei</em> — the register distinction, formally taught. Interrogative adverbs and pronouns: <em>come, quando, chi, che cosa, quanto, dove, perché</em>.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Language</span></div><div>Greetings and forms of address in formal and informal settings.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Can-do</span></div><div>Hold the same short exchange twice, once with a friend and once with a shopkeeper, changing the language appropriately.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Task</span></div><div>The same three questions asked both ways, recorded.</div></div>
</div>
<Warn lead={"Worth a full unit even though it looks small"}>
<p>Register is explicitly marked in the role play and in two of the writing questions, and getting it
  wrong caps the mark regardless of how good the Italian is. Establishing it early means it never has
  to be retrofitted.</p>
</Warn>

<Section kicker={"Block D"}>First transactions · Units 13–14</Section>

<h3>Unit 13 — Buying and ordering</h3>
<div class="spec">
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Grammar</span></div><div>The partitive (<em>del, della, dei, delle</em>). <em>Ecco</em>. <em>Vorrei</em> — the one conditional form they need immediately, taught as a fixed phrase for now rather than as a tense.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Language</span></div><div>Food and drink; meals; quantities; shops; money; ordering in a café or restaurant.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Can-do</span></div><div>Order food, ask the price, buy a quantity of something.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Task</span></div><div>First proper role play. You play the waiter or shopkeeper, in Italian throughout, formal register. Recorded.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Links</span></div><div>Theme 1 — food and drink; shopping. Theme 2 — eating out; shopping.</div></div>
</div>

<h3>Unit 14 — Consolidation and first assessment</h3>
<div class="spec">
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Grammar</span></div><div>Nothing new. Everything from Units 1–13, mixed.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Language</span></div><div>All fields so far.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Can-do</span></div><div>Sustain two minutes of conversation about themselves, their family, their interests and their daily life.</div></div>
  <div class="row"><div class="badges"><span class="rowlbl">Task</span></div><div>Three pieces: a written piece of 40–50 words about themselves; a two-minute recorded conversation with no notes; ten English sentences to translate into Italian.</div></div>
</div>
<Note lead={"How to mark it"}>
<p>The writing against the Foundation Question 1 grid in the spec, the speaking against the Foundation
  conversation grids. Not to produce a grade — they will score low and that is expected this early —
  but so that the standard is familiar rather than shocking in two years' time.</p>
</Note>

<Section kicker={"After Stage 1"}>What comes next</Section>

<p>Stage 2, Units 15 onwards, moves into the past: the perfect tense with both auxiliaries, reflexive
verbs, and object pronouns. That is the point at which Italian stops being a vocabulary exercise and
starts being a language, and it is also where the two children are most likely to diverge in pace.</p>

<p>Worth building Stage 2 once you have seen how Stage 1 actually goes, rather than now.</p>
