<Section kicker="Session 1">The point</Section>

<p>Every Italian noun is either masculine or feminine. Not the thing — the <em>word</em>. A chair is
not feminine in any way you could point at; <em>sedia</em> simply is a feminine word, and that fact
travels with it everywhere.</p>

<Rule lead="The last letter tells you, most of the time">
<WordTable words={[{"term": "-o → masculine → il", "say": "eel", "gloss": "<em>il libro</em>, <em>il tavolo</em>"}, {"term": "-a → feminine → la", "say": "lah", "gloss": "<em>la sedia</em>, <em>la penna</em>"}, {"term": "-e → could be either", "say": "", "gloss": "has to be learned: <em>il cane</em>, <em>la classe</em>"}]} />
  <Lead spaced>So there are two jobs, and only the first one is a rule</Lead>
  <p>Look at the last letter, and you know the gender. Know the gender, and the article follows
  with no thought at all. The nouns ending in <em>-e</em> are the ones that need memorising, and
  there are far fewer of them.</p>
</Rule>

<h3>How to teach it</h3>

<Steps>
<li>Say the rule and write it down. Two lines, and it goes on the fifth page of the notebook under the heading <strong>ARTICLES</strong>.</li>
  <li>Take these sixteen words one at a time, out loud. For each one: last letter, then gender, then article, then the whole thing said together. <Grid words={["libro", "sedia", "tavolo", "penna", "quaderno", "cucina", "letto", "finestra", "bagno", "matita", "giardino", "lavagna", "divano", "gomma", "banco", "porta"]} /></li>
  <li>Then go round again saying only the finished pair — <em>il libro</em>, <em>la sedia</em> — until there is no gap between the article and the noun. The gap is the thing you are removing.</li>
</Steps>

<Notebook lead="In the notebook">
<p>The rule first, on the fifth page:</p>
  <WriteOut>
nouns ending in <strong>-o</strong> are usually <strong>masculine</strong> → <strong>il</strong><br />
    nouns ending in <strong>-a</strong> are usually <strong>feminine</strong> → <strong>la</strong><br />
    nouns ending in <strong>-e</strong> can be either — learn it with the noun
</WriteOut>
  <p>Then two columns, headed <strong>IL</strong> and <strong>LA</strong>, and the sixteen words
  sorted into them — written out in full, article included, never the bare noun.</p>
</Notebook>

<Note lead="The two that are not decided by the rule">
<p><em>Il cane</em> and <em>la classe</em> both end in <em>-e</em>, so the last letter tells you
nothing and the article has to be remembered along with the word. Put them at the bottom of the
page with a line above them. There will be more of these; that line is where they go.</p>
</Note>

<Answers lead="Answers">
<p><strong>il</strong> — libro, tavolo, quaderno, letto, bagno, giardino, divano, banco.<br />
  <strong>la</strong> — sedia, penna, cucina, finestra, matita, lavagna, gomma, porta.</p>
</Answers>

<DoneWhen lead="Done when">
<p>They can take any of the sixteen and produce the pair without a pause, and say which letter told
them. A pause means the noun was learned alone and the article is being worked out each time, which
is the habit this session exists to prevent.</p>
</DoneWhen>
