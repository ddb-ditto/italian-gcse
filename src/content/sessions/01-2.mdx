<Section kicker={"Session 2"}>The point</Section>

<p><em>C</em> and <em>g</em> change sound depending on the letter that follows.</p>

<Rule lead={"Before a, o, u → hard"}>
<WordTable words={[{"term": "casa", "say": "KA-za", "gloss": "hard c"}, {"term": "gatto", "say": "GAT-to", "gloss": "hard g"}, {"term": "cura, gusto", "say": "KU-ra, GUS-to", "gloss": "same again"}]} />
  <Lead spaced>Before e, i → soft</Lead>
  <WordTable words={[{"term": "cena", "say": "CHE-na", "gloss": "c becomes \"ch\" as in <em>chair</em>"}, {"term": "cinema", "say": "CHEE-ne-ma", "gloss": ""}, {"term": "gelato", "say": "je-LA-to", "gloss": "g becomes \"j\" as in <em>jam</em>"}, {"term": "giro", "say": "JEE-ro", "gloss": ""}]} />
  <Lead spaced>H forces it back to hard</Lead>
  <WordTable words={[{"term": "chi, che", "say": "kee, kay", "gloss": "the h is never pronounced"}, {"term": "spaghetti", "say": "spa-GET-tee", "gloss": "its only job is to keep c and g hard"}]} />
  <Lead spaced>I forces it soft before a, o, u — and is not itself pronounced</Lead>
  <WordTable words={[{"term": "ciao", "say": "chow", "gloss": "not \"chee-ow\""}, {"term": "giornale", "say": "jor-NA-le", "gloss": ""}]} />
</Rule>

<h3>How to teach it</h3>
<Steps>
<li>Write the rule out as a simple table and have them copy it. This one is worth writing down.</li>
  <li>Sort these words into hard or soft, out loud, one at a time: <Grid words={["cane", "cena", "gatto", "gelato", "chiesa", "ciao", "cinque", "cosa", "giovane", "gonna", "ghiaccio", "chilo", "cucina", "cioccolato", "magico", "spaghetti"]} /></li>
  <li>Explain <em>ciao</em> properly. They already know the word; they have probably never noticed the i is silent. That is a nice moment.</li>
</Steps>

<Notebook lead={"In the notebook"}>
<p>The rule goes in first, at the top of a fresh page, in their own handwriting:</p>
  <WriteOut>
c and g are <strong>hard</strong> before a, o, u<br />
    c and g are <strong>soft</strong> before e, i<br />
    <strong>h</strong> after c or g forces it hard — and is never pronounced<br />
    <strong>i</strong> after c or g forces it soft — and is not pronounced either
</WriteOut>
  <p>Then rule two columns, headed <strong>HARD</strong> and <strong>SOFT</strong>, and sort the sixteen
  words into them — writing each word out in full rather than listing it, and
  <strong>circling the letter that decides</strong>: the vowel after the c or g, or the h that overrides
  it.</p>
  <p><em>Cucina</em> belongs in both columns. Don't tell them — let them get stuck on it and work out
  why. That one word does more for the rule than the other fifteen together.</p>
</Notebook>

<Answers lead={"Answers"}>
<p><strong>Hard</strong> — cane, gatto, chiesa, cinque, cosa, gonna, ghiaccio, chilo, cucina, spaghetti.<br />
  <strong>Soft</strong> — cena, gelato, ciao, giovane, cioccolato, magico.<br />
  Note <em>cucina</em> has both: hard <em>cu</em>, soft <em>ci</em>.</p>
</Answers>

<Note lead={"Why it matters later"}>
<p>This rule is why verb spellings change — <em>cerco</em> but <em>cerchi</em>, <em>pago</em> but
  <em>paghi</em>. When that comes up in Unit 6 you will be able to point back here rather than teaching
  it as an arbitrary exception.</p>
</Note>

<DoneWhen lead={"Done when"}>
<p>They can state the rule in their own words — not just follow it when reminded — and sort the
  sixteen words without help. This is the can-do the whole unit turns on.</p>
</DoneWhen>
