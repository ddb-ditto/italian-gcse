<Section kicker={"The big one"}>Double consonants</Section>

<p>A double consonant in Italian is genuinely a longer sound, not a spelling convention. It changes
meaning:</p>

<WordTable pairs words={[{"term": "nonno", "say": "grandfather", "gloss": ""}, {"term": "nono", "say": "ninth", "gloss": ""}, {"term": "cane", "say": "dog", "gloss": ""}, {"term": "canne", "say": "reeds", "gloss": ""}, {"term": "sono", "say": "I am", "gloss": ""}, {"term": "sonno", "say": "sleep", "gloss": ""}, {"term": "casa", "say": "house", "gloss": ""}, {"term": "cassa", "say": "crate, till", "gloss": ""}, {"term": "copia", "say": "copy", "gloss": ""}, {"term": "coppia", "say": "couple", "gloss": ""}]} />

<p>How to make it: hold the consonant. For <em>nonno</em>, stop on the N for a beat before releasing.
For <em>pizza</em>, the ts sound is stretched.</p>

<Notebook lead={"In the notebook"}>
<p>Three short entries, each with its examples and a note underneath in their own words:</p>
  <WriteOut>
gn — gnocchi, signore &nbsp;·&nbsp; one sound, like ny in canyon<br />
    gli — famiglia, figlio &nbsp;·&nbsp; like lli in million<br />
    sc — pesce (sh), scuola (sk) &nbsp;·&nbsp; same rule as c and g
</WriteOut>
  <p>Then the five pairs, written as pairs with their meanings, and the doubled letter
  <strong>underlined twice</strong>:</p>
  <WriteOut>
nonno (grandfather) / nono (ninth)
</WriteOut>
  <p>The double underline is the reminder that the letter is <em>held</em>, not merely written twice.
  It is the difference between a spelling and a sound.</p>
</Notebook>

<h3>How to teach it</h3>
<p>Say pairs from the list above and have them tell you which one you said. Then swap — they say, you
guess.</p>

<Note lead={"For a younger child"} child>
<p>Run this as a game rather than a drill. Guessing which one you said, and catching you out when
  they take their turn, holds attention far better than repetition does.</p>
</Note>

<DoneWhen lead={"Done when"}>
<p>They can hear the difference between <em>nonno</em> and <em>nono</em> reliably. Recognisable
  <em>gli</em> is enough — do not stall on it. If the double consonants are not landing, repeat this
  session before moving on; everything downstream depends on hearing that distinction.</p>
</DoneWhen>
