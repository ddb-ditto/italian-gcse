<Section kicker={"Session 4"}>The point</Section>

<p>Most Italian words are stressed on the second-to-last syllable.</p>
<Grid words={["ca-SA", "a-MI-co", "si-GNO-re", "bi-ci-CLET-ta", "spa-GHET-ti"]} />

<p>When a word is stressed on the final syllable, it carries a written accent so you always know:</p>
<Grid words={["città", "perché", "caffè", "università", "papà", "lunedì"]} />

<p>A minority are stressed earlier and have no marking. These just have to be learned:</p>
<Grid words={["TA-vo-lo", "MU-si-ca", "SA-ba-to", "TE-le-fo-no", "dif-FI-ci-le"]} />

<h3>How to teach it</h3>
<Steps>
<li>Clap the syllables of words from the previous sessions, clapping harder on the stressed one.</li>
  <li>Give them a list of unfamiliar words and have them predict the stress before you say it. They should be right most of the time, which is the point — the default is reliable.</li>
  <li>Note that stress can distinguish meaning: <em>PARlo</em> (I speak) versus <em>parLÒ</em> (he spoke). Don't explain the tenses; just show that it matters.</li>
</Steps>

<Notebook lead={"In the notebook"}>
<p>This session is mostly writing, and the writing <em>is</em> the exercise. Three headed lists. In
  each, they copy the word, break it into syllables with hyphens, and capitalise the stressed one.</p>
  <WriteOut>
<strong>The normal way</strong> — ca-SA, a-MI-co, si-GNO-re, bi-ci-CLET-ta, spa-GHET-ti<br />
    <strong>Marked with an accent</strong> — cit-TÀ, per-CHÉ, caf-FÈ, u-ni-ver-si-TÀ, pa-PÀ, lu-ne-DÌ<br />
    <strong>Just learn these</strong> — TA-vo-lo, MU-si-ca, SA-ba-to, TE-le-fo-no, dif-FI-ci-le
</WriteOut>
  <p>Make them write the accents themselves rather than skipping them. Getting <em>à è é ì ò ù</em> onto
  paper is worth the fuss now — a missing accent is a spelling mistake in the written papers, and this
  is the only session where the accent is the subject rather than a detail.</p>
</Notebook>

<Note lead={"Common problem"}>
<p>They apply the default to the accented words and say <em>ci-TTA</em> as <em>CIT-ta</em>. The accent
  is not decoration — it is there precisely because the word breaks the rule.</p>
</Note>

<DoneWhen lead={"Done when"}>
<p>Given an unfamiliar word with no accent, they put the stress on the second-to-last syllable
  without being prompted — and when they see an accent, they move it to the end.</p>
</DoneWhen>
